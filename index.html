Vernacular teaches frontier assistants the native language of a product’s files, then keeps a record of what was tested, on which version, and what still fails.

Why this exists

People keep their tools

Years of presets, projects and settings live inside products people trust. Few will abandon them to start again inside a chat window.

General assistants are the strongest

A frontier model that understands a product usually beats a specialised AI built for it. What it lacks is the product’s language.

Fluency needs proof

A file that looks right can still be wrong. Every claim here is tested against the real product, and every gap is written down.

How a tool is learned

  1. Change one thing

    Save the same file twice with a single setting changed. The difference is the first word of the language.

  2. Propose a meaning

    The assistant decodes the file, separates the real change from noise such as IDs and names, and says what it thinks the change means.

  3. Test it on the product

    It designs the experiment that would prove it wrong. The owner loads the result and reports what the product shows.

  4. Keep the record

    What survives becomes a skill any assistant can load, with the evidence attached, ready to re-test when the product updates.
